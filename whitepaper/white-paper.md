# Cinnamaldehyde variation across cinnamon types: evidence and measurement plan

**Study status:** scoping only; no primary measurement dataset assembled.

## Abstract

The source project proposes that cinnamaldehyde concentration differs between Ceylon (*Cinnamomum verum*) and Cassia cinnamon. Its research note reports ranges for essential oil and ground powder but does not provide a study-level citation trail or raw observations. These values are therefore recorded as unverified narrative claims, not pooled estimates or new results.

## Current source claims

The source note reports 50–75% cinnamaldehyde in Ceylon essential oil and 70–95% in Cassia essential oil. For ground powder it reports 0.5–2.0% and 1.5–4.0% by weight, respectively. Those four ranges are transcribed in `data/source_claims.json`. The source note names Rao & Gan (2014) generally but does not tie individual ranges to specific papers, samples, analytical methods, or citations.

## Measurement plan

A defensible comparison requires authenticated species and plant part, origin and harvest metadata, processing/storage history, replicated lots, standardized extraction, calibrated GC-MS or GC-FID quantitation with an internal standard, limits of detection/quantitation, and reporting on both dry-mass and essential-oil bases where relevant. Study-level measurements should retain assay method and uncertainty. Percent composition of an essential oil must not be conflated with percent concentration in bark powder.

## Conclusion

The existing materials support a research question and a hypothesis, not a verified quantitative conclusion. No statistical comparison is possible until source studies are resolved or new samples are measured. The attached notebook makes this boundary explicit and checks only the internal unit conversion of the transcribed ranges.
